Preparing for Your Antenatal Care Appointment

Antenatal appointments are an opportunity to review your pregnancy and discuss the care you may need as it progresses. Your GP may ask about your medical history, previous pregnancies where relevant, current medications and any symptoms or changes you have experienced. Depending on your stage of pregnancy and individual circumstances, discussions may also cover recommended blood tests, ultrasound scans, health assessments and referrals to other maternity care providers.

Each visit can help you better understand what to expect in the weeks ahead and prepare for upcoming stages of your pregnancy. At Everbright Medical, we encourage you to bring your questions and share anything you would like to discuss with your GP. Whether you are arranging your first antenatal appointment or attending a follow-up consultation, we aim to make your visits welcoming, informative and focused on your individual healthcare needs in Kurri Kurri, NSW.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is antenatal care?

Antenatal care refers to the healthcare and support provided during pregnancy. It involves regular consultations with healthcare professionals to review your health, discuss your baby's development, identify potential concerns and help you prepare for the different stages of pregnancy.

When should I book my first antenatal appointment?

It is recommended to arrange your first antenatal appointment early in pregnancy, ideally around 6–8 weeks. Early consultations allow your GP to review your health, discuss pregnancy care options and help plan any recommended checks or referrals.

What happens during an antenatal appointment?

Your antenatal appointment may involve discussing your medical history, current medications, pregnancy symptoms and general wellbeing. Depending on your individual needs and stage of pregnancy, your GP may also discuss recommended health checks, blood tests, ultrasound scans and referrals to appropriate maternity services.

How often should I attend antenatal appointments?

The frequency of antenatal appointments depends on your stage of pregnancy, medical history and individual healthcare needs. Your GP or maternity care team can discuss an appropriate appointment schedule and any additional consultations that may be recommended.

Do I need blood tests or ultrasound scans during pregnancy?

Blood tests and ultrasound scans are commonly recommended at different stages of pregnancy, depending on individual circumstances. Your GP can discuss which investigations may be appropriate, explain their purpose and arrange referrals where necessary.
